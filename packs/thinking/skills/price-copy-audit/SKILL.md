# Price and copy audit

Clean-room rewrite: no third-party text was copied into this skill. The pricing-strategy and copywriting checklists below are standard, widely-published practice (value-based pricing, anchoring, hook-first copy), assembled here as one combined pass rather than adapted from any single source.

## What this checks

Run pricing and copy together, because a good price badly explained undersells, and good copy over a confusing price structure still loses the sale.

### Pricing pass

1. Is there a clear anchor (a higher-priced option shown first, or a "most popular" tier) or does every tier look equally weighted?
2. Does each tier's price map to a specific, nameable difference in what's delivered — not just "more of the same"?
3. Is the cheapest option positioned to make the middle tier the obvious choice, or does it look like the best deal on its own?
4. Are there any prices stated as a range or "starting from" that will surprise the customer later? Flag for clarity.

### Copy pass

1. Does the first line on the page lead with the reader's problem/outcome, or with the company's description of itself?
2. Is there a specific claim (a number, a name, a timeframe) in the first screen, or only general language?
3. Does the copy read aloud at a natural pace, or does it stack qualifiers and jargon?
4. Is there one clear next action per section, or several competing calls to action?

## Steps

1. Read the whole page once before scoring anything.
2. Score each pricing and copy question above with a one-line reason.
3. Rank the three highest-impact fixes (not every issue found) — a launch-blocking pricing gap and a scroll-stopping first line matter more than a comma.
4. Propose a specific rewrite for the top fix, not just a description of the problem.

## Worked example

Page has three tiers: RM500, RM1,200, RM2,500 — but the RM1,200 tier lists no different deliverable than the RM500 tier except "priority support."
Fix: name one concrete extra deliverable for the middle tier, or drop it and let the two real tiers stand.

First line: "We are a full-service agency specializing in digital transformation solutions."
Fix: lead with the outcome the reader wants, with a specific number where possible — see `feature-to-feeling` for the feature-to-felt-value rewrite method.

## Failure modes

- Do not recommend a price change as a "pricing fix" — that's a business decision for the owner; this skill flags framing and clarity issues, not what to charge.
- Do not list every stylistic nitpick as equally urgent — rank by likely impact on the sale.
- Do not assume AU/UK/US pricing psychology norms apply unchanged to a different market; note the audience before scoring anchoring and framing.

## Human approval

This skill produces an audit and draft rewrites only. Publishing a new price or new page copy is the owner's action — this skill has no publish action of its own.
