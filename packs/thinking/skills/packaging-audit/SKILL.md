# Packaging audit

Packaging (title + thumbnail together) is most of what decides whether someone clicks — the video itself decides whether they finish watching. This skill audits the click, not the content.

One rubric works across channels; only the tone ceiling (how bold a title can be) and the thumbnail look change per audience.

## Before you start

Name the channel's profile in one line: audience, and how far the title can push (a corporate B2B channel tolerates less hype than a viral how-to channel).

## The rubric

Score each 1–5, with a one-line reason:

1. **Glance test** — does the thumbnail read in under a second at small size (phone, sidebar)?
2. **Curiosity gap** — does the title+thumbnail pair raise a question the video answers, without giving the answer away?
3. **Specificity** — is there a concrete number, name or outcome, versus a vague claim?
4. **Contrast** — does the thumbnail stand out against what else is in the feed for this audience (not just against itself)?
5. **Promise match** — does the video actually deliver what the title/thumbnail imply? A packaging trick that doesn't pay off tanks future videos' trust.

## Steps

1. Score all five against the pair as given.
2. Flag the lowest-scoring one as the priority fix.
3. Propose 2–3 alternate titles and one thumbnail text/composition change that would move that score, staying inside the channel's tone ceiling.
4. If diagnosing a live video's poor CTR, check the promise-match score first — a low-CTR video with strong packaging on paper is more likely a promise-match or audience-fit issue than a "make it punchier" issue.

## Worked example

Title: "How I Cleaned My House" — Thumbnail: plain photo of a clean room.
Scores: glance 3, curiosity 1 (no question raised), specificity 1, contrast 2, promise-match n/a (untested).
Fix: "The 20-Minute Rule That Keeps My House Clean (No Deep-Cleaning Days)" + thumbnail with a big "20 MIN" and a visible before/after split.

## Failure modes

- Do not chase curiosity at the cost of promise-match — a title that overpromises trains viewers to distrust the channel.
- Do not copy another channel's exact packaging style without checking it fits this channel's tone ceiling.
- A low score on "specificity" is not fixed by making the title longer — it's fixed by finding the actual concrete detail in the video.

## Human approval

This skill scores and drafts alternatives only. Publishing the video, uploading the new thumbnail, or changing a live title is the owner's action — this skill has no publish or upload action of its own.
