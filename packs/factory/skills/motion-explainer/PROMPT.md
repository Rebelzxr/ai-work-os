# Copy-paste edition

## What to give it

- The finished, fact-checked script: `[SCRIPT]`
- What the viewer should understand at the end: `[ONE SENTENCE]`
- Format: `[LONG 16:9 or SHORT 9:16]`
- The proof you may show (own screenshots, own numbers with their source, licensed clips): `[PROOF LIST]`
- Clean it locally first: no client names, phone numbers, account IDs or anything private.

## The prompt

```text
You are a motion designer planning an HTML explainer video. Do not write code and do not render.

SCRIPT:
[SCRIPT]

GOAL: [ONE SENTENCE]
FORMAT: [LONG 16:9 or SHORT 9:16]
PROOF I CAN SHOW: [PROOF LIST]

Produce four sections:
1. STYLE: one base colour, and every other colour with one meaning (keyword, tool, problem, pass). Text at least 5% of frame height in 16:9, at least 60 px on a 1080x1920 Short. Real logos only from brand assets. Never AI-generated logos or real faces.
2. SCENES: a table with time span, narration, on screen, one motion verb, on-screen text, proof asset. Change the screen every 2-4 s (long) or 1.5-2.5 s (Short). One group visible at a time. Proof fills at least 60% of the width with a source and date label. Any number without a source in my proof list becomes a plain sentence or is removed, and you say so.
3. CHAPTER BRIEFS: one per chapter, with its scenes, the exact narration and what "done" means.
4. REVIEW CHECKLIST: the questions a different reviewer must answer before anyone uploads it.
For a Short: frame 1 is a number or a visible result, nothing important in the bottom 350 px or right 150 px, no end card, the end loops to frame 1.
For a long video, hold the final frame at least 10 seconds with space for end-screen elements. This is exempt from the 2-4 second screen-change rule.
```

## What you get back

A locked style, a scene table, one brief per chapter and a review checklist. No code and no video file.

## Check before you use it

- Every number on screen traces back to your proof list.
- No AI-generated logo or face, and nothing private on screen.
- Each scene has one motion verb and the timings add up to the script length.
- Someone other than the builder reviews the finished video before it goes out.

## Next job

Give the chapter briefs to your builder or video tool. After the build, give a different AI the review checklist, the style, the scene table, the script, the captions, the proof sources, the contact sheets and, if it can take them, the rendered video and a sound measurement report. Anything it could not watch or measure counts as UNVERIFIED, not passed.
