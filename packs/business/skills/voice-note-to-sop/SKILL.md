# Voice note to SOP

Adapted from Dainer's published library guide "Turn a rambling voice note into a step-by-step SOP" (<https://dainer-ai.biz/library>).

## What this needs

- A transcript of the owner explaining one job end-to-end (opening the shop, receiving a customer's item, packing an order, closing the till). The transcript may be messy, out of order, or repeat itself — that is expected.

## Steps

1. Read the whole transcript before writing anything.
2. Reorder into the actual sequence the job happens in, even if the owner explained it out of order.
3. Write numbered steps using only what was said — add no step, no tool and no rule the owner did not mention.
4. Pull out anything said as a warning or a mistake ("don't forget to...", "last time someone...") into a separate "Common mistakes" section.
5. Anywhere the owner was vague, unclear or contradicted themselves, list it as an **open question** instead of guessing a resolution.
6. End with a version line: draft number, date, and "not yet tested with staff."

## Worked example

Transcript fragment: "...oh and before you close the till you gotta count the cash twice, I always forget to mention that, people mess it up all the time..."
→ Step: "Count the cash twice before closing the till."
→ Common mistake: "Staff often forget to count twice — this is the most common till error."

Transcript fragment: "...pack it up, use the small box unless it's, I don't know, unless it's a big order I guess..."
→ Open question: "What counts as a 'big order' that needs a different box size? Owner did not give a threshold."

## Failure modes

- Never invent a step, quantity or tool not mentioned in the transcript.
- Never resolve an owner's vague or contradictory statement by picking the version that "sounds right" — list it as an open question.
- Do not skip the staff test — a written SOP that has not been tried by someone who does not know the job is still a draft, not version 1.

## Human approval

This skill produces a written draft SOP for internal use. It does not publish, distribute or email the SOP anywhere — the owner reviews it, tests it with staff, and decides where it lives.
