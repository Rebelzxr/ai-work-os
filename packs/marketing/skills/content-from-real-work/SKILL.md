# Content from real work

A generic method for turning one piece of real, already-sanitised work into shareable drafts. This is the method only — not any private voice, tone or skill text. If you have your own personal-brand voice skill, use that instead; this one is for anyone starting from nothing.

## What this produces

From one sanitised job, project or receipt: a LinkedIn-length draft, a short X/social-length draft, and a longer blog-length draft, plus a one-line and a three-line repurposed version of each for reuse elsewhere. All drafts describe a real method or real result — nothing invented, no client identifiable.

## Before you start

- One real job, project or receipt, already sanitised: no client name, no company name unless the client agreed, no numbers that could only belong to one identifiable deal.
- The one useful thing this piece of work taught or proved — a method, a mistake avoided, a number that moved, a before/after.
- Which platform matters most right now (pick one to lead with; the others are repurposed from it, not written separately from scratch).

**Privacy first:** remove client names, contact details and identifying deal details before sharing the note. If you installed the optional `aiwos-business` pack, use its `safe-to-paste` skill to help with this check. If the client's identity can be inferred even without their name (a very specific industry, city and deal size together), generalise one of those details before drafting.

## Steps

1. **Extract the one useful thing.** Not "we did a project" — the specific decision, method or number: "we cut X by doing Y", "the mistake was Z, the fix was W".
2. **Draft the long form first** (LinkedIn or blog, whichever the owner leads with): a short story with a concrete before, what was tried, and any supported result or lesson. When the note gives only a method, describe that method; ask the owner for a result or leave a [placeholder] instead of inventing one.
3. **Cut the short form** (X or similar) from the same real content — the sharpest single sentence or two from the long draft, not a separate invented hook.
4. **Repurpose down**: a one-line version (for a caption or a follow-up message) and a three-line version (for a comment or a shorter post), both built only from claims already in the long draft.
5. **Output**: long draft, short draft, one-line version, three-line version — each labelled with which platform it targets.

## Worked example

Real work (sanitised): "Local service business, RM enquiries via WhatsApp were getting missed overnight. Built a simple lead-triage sort so enquiries get sorted into Hot/Warm/Later each morning before the owner even opens the phone."

→ LinkedIn draft: "WhatsApp enquiries were getting missed overnight at a local service business. We built a morning sort that puts them into Hot, Warm or Later before the owner opens the phone. [Owner: add a measured result or lesson, if available.]"
→ Short draft: "We built a morning sort for a local service business: WhatsApp enquiries go into Hot, Warm or Later before the owner opens the phone."
→ One-line: "A morning sort puts WhatsApp enquiries into Hot, Warm or Later."
→ Three-line:
> WhatsApp enquiries were getting missed overnight.<br>
> We built a morning sort into Hot, Warm or Later.<br>
> The enquiries are sorted before the owner opens the phone.

→ Owner check: the note gives no measured result, duration, customer quote or owner lesson. Supply those details before adding any such claim; otherwise keep the drafts about the method alone.

## Failure modes

- Do not name a client, company or specific person without their explicit prior agreement, recorded in the input.
- Do not invent a number, a quote or a result the real work does not support.
- Do not write the short and repurposed versions as fresh invented hooks — they must be cut from claims already present in the long draft.
- Do not copy another person's or brand's distinctive voice, phrasing or private skill text — this is a generic method, not a style transplant.

## Human approval

**This skill only produces draft text. A person reviews every draft and posts, publishes or sends it themselves on their own account — this skill never posts, publishes or connects to any social or CMS account.**
